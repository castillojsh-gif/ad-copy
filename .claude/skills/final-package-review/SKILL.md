# Final Package Review

This skill is the last quality gate before an ad copy package is delivered. Run every pass below in order. Do it slowly and deliberately. Fix what fails, then verify the fix didn't break anything nearby.

## How to Run This Review

Work through the six passes in sequence. Facts come first because a factual failure invalidates everything else. Do not skim. Read every asset in the package for every pass.

---

## PASS 1 — Claim Integrity & Market Accuracy

The most expensive failures. Check these before touching style.

- **No fabrication.** No invented case studies, statistics, client results, or named wins. If a number or result isn't in the client materials, it doesn't go in the copy.
- **Pressure-test every claim** against how the business actually works. Kill fragile assumptions dressed up as insight (e.g., seasonality claims that aren't true across the whole niche, or role claims like "front desk delivers care" that don't match reality).
- **Claims must hold across the entire target market.** If the niche spans multiple practice or business types, the pain must be written at the level they all share. Cut examples that only speak to one sub-segment.
- **When credibility is thin, build an honest bridge from what's real.** Don't invent wins. Cut credentials that don't actually help the pitch.
- **Flag document conflicts instead of guessing.** Number mismatches, revenue qualifier mismatches, conflicting guarantee wording. Surface them; never silently pick one.
- **Social proof uses first names only.** Never full names.

## PASS 2 — Substance & Angle

- **Benefits and outcomes before features.** Lead with what the prospect's world looks like after the system runs, not the mechanism. What does a Monday morning feel like when this is working?
- **Preserve the curiosity gap.** Name pillars at intrigue level only, one outcome-led sentence per pillar. Never reveal how the system works. If the prospect could DIY it after reading, too much was given away. The "how" belongs on the sales call.
- **Every sentence earns its place.** Cut throat-clears, filler, and lines that don't matter to what the ideal prospect actually wants. Client documents are raw material, not gospel; the copy serves the prospect, not the document.
- **Never generic.** If a line could run for a roofer and a surgeon, it's too vague. Every asset, especially headlines, must be unmistakably about this niche.
- **Distinct angles per asset.** Each script addresses a genuinely different pain point or pillar. Script 1 and Script 3 must not overlap. No two assets open the same way or lean on the same phrasing.
- **Client-flagged differentiators are featured** where the client asked for them (e.g., a real human concierge, financial pre-screening), and specific CTAs are matched to the specific assets requested (e.g., free audit vs. book a call).

## PASS 3 — Guarantee & Compliance

- **Guarantee language is identical across every asset** in the package.
- **Always tied to its condition, never standalone.** Never "we work for free" on its own. Approved constructions: "you don't pay," "it's free," "it's free until we do," "you don't pay until we deliver," "or we keep working at no additional cost until we do."
- **Student clients:** only "you don't pay," "it's free," or "we cover your marketing costs."
- **Contingencies stay off the ad.** Spending minimums, appointment slot requirements, and other fine print live on the sales call. The ad's only job is the booked call.
- **No platform names** (Facebook, Instagram, Meta, Google, or any ad platform) in consumer-facing copy.
- **No swipes at the category or competitors,** especially for delicate avatars. Contrast comes from what's offered, not from knocking others.
- **Sales-call language stays off ads** (e.g., "first month is on us").

## PASS 4 — Voice & Phrasing

- **Tribal, niche-specific language.** The words the prospect uses for themselves and their work: Seller Central, dispatch board, techs, cases, consults, patients. Never the dashboard-metric or agency version. Never marketing jargon.
- **5th grade reading level throughout,** even for sophisticated audiences. Simple words beat impressive ones.
- **Simplify hard-to-say sentences.** Read them out loud. Break one long packed sentence into two shorter ones.
- **Concrete over vague.** Swap clumsy or abstract words for specific ones the prospect cares about ("numbers" becomes "profit").
- **Cut hedge words** when they soften a line before it lands: maybe, usually, eventually, really, just, sort of.
- **Market callout in the first two lines** of every script and primary text, varied per asset rather than copy-pasted.
- **Vary openers and recurring beats** across the package and across clients. Rotate future-state entry lines and repeat phrases so no two assets or packages read identically.
- **Rewrite the credential/proof line distinctly in every asset.** Same facts, different construction, so no two assets clone each other when they run side by side.
- **Ideas flow.** Sentences connect and build naturally into each other; nothing choppy or robotic.
- **Numbers format:** "30+" not "30 plus"; "$60K" not "$60,000" unless the full figure adds impact. Consistent within a package.

## PASS 5 — Robotic-Pattern Sweep

Hunt for and eliminate every instance of these patterns:

- Em dashes (replace with commas or restructure)
- Tricolons: exactly three parallel items used as a rhetorical device
- "Not X. It's Y." / "No X. Just Y." two-beat contrasts
- Staccato fragment stacks (3+ short fragments in a row)
- "Here's the..." setups and similar reveal phrases
- Preachy "Most..." generalizations; let the reader self-identify instead of being told what they do
- Orphaned dramatic one-liners that exist purely for emphasis
- Mic-drop lines after the CTA
- Consecutive paragraphs opening with the same word
- Artificial momentum phrases ("the best part," "it gets better")

**Then do a second sweep.** Check whether any rewrite from this pass introduced a subtler version of the same pattern, a new "Here's" setup, a new contrast, a new fragment stack, or a new dramatic closer.

## PASS 6 — Formatting & Delivery

- **Paragraphs are 1-2 sentences max** with generous line spacing. A wall of text fails on mobile. If a section feels dense, break it up.
- **Clean, copy-paste-ready output:** client name and system name as the header, niche below it, each asset clearly labeled and separated by a divider.
- **No editorial commentary,** revision notes, or bracketed instructions inside the final output.
- **Deliver the full package in one pass** when asked.

---

## Editing Rules (How to Apply Fixes)

- **Retain what's working.** Change only what failed a check, then confirm nothing around it drifted.
- **Blend every swapped line** into the surrounding copy so it reads consistently, not like a patch.
- **Apply every correction everywhere it occurs,** not just where it was spotted. One flagged line means checking the whole package for the same issue.
- **On revision requests, make only the change asked for** (applied package-wide), then re-run Pass 5 before returning.

## Final Confirmation Before Returning

Before delivering, confirm all of the following are true:

1. Zero fabricated or unverifiable claims
2. Guarantee identical in every asset, tied to its condition
3. No platform names, no contingencies, no competitor swipes
4. Every asset opens differently and covers a distinct angle
5. Pattern sweep passed twice, including the post-edit sweep
6. Formatting is mobile-ready and copy-paste clean

If any item fails, fix it and re-check before delivery.
